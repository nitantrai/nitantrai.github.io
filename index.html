02 / RESEARCH

Measuring a changing forest.

Dissertation: Remote Sensing and Machine Learning Applications for Forest Monitoring

01

Reconstructing Tree Height and DBH from Partial Stem Profiles Using Inverse Taper Models for Loblolly Pine

This research investigates reconstructing tree height and diameter at breast height (DBH) from partial stem profiles of loblolly pine using inverse taper models. It examines model robustness under incomplete measurement scenarios, including missing height and DBH, to support forest measurements when complete tree data are unavailable.

02

Unified Modeling Approach for Predicting Basal Area and Total Stem Volume Across LiDAR Densities

This research investigates a unified modeling approach for predicting basal area and total stem volume across LiDAR densities. It combines LiDAR-derived metrics with field inventory measurements to examine forest attribute predictions as data density varies, with attention to model validation and uncertainty.

03

Deep Learning-Based UAV Seedling Detection Workflow

This research develops deep learning workflows to detect and count tree seedlings in multispectral UAV imagery for forest regeneration monitoring. It evaluates YOLOv12 and D-FINE across field conditions and produces batch inference outputs, CSV summaries, and visualizations to support repeatable monitoring.
